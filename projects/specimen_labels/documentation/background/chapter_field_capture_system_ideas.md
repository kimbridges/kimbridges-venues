## Threads
1. **Capture yields THREE things, bound by an ID tag (Kim, 2026-07-02).** Reframed from "two
   products" to an act of **capture** that comes away with three: the **specimen** (the pressed
   plant), the **label**, and the **field log**. One physical, two written. The **specimen
   carries a small ID tag (a unique number)** that ties the plant to its label, its log, and
   everything attached later. (This quietly introduces the linking key we lean on in Ch. 4
   "capture once, project two views" and Ch. 5's identity deck. Don't over-explain it here; just
   plant it.)
2. **Which record is lost.** The specimen is kept and the label usually gets made; the **log**
   is the one that vanishes, and it's the log that carries the context the modern (indicator)
   uses depend on. "We lose the half we've come to need most."
3. **Friction made concrete — the old kit.** The 1970s instrument kit, instrument by instrument
   (altimeter, clinometer, compass, topo map, notebook), and the notebook's miseries
   (write-in-the-rain book, pencils, the knife to sharpen them, writing with no flat surface,
   illegible later). First-person, remembered "in my hands." NOTE (Kim): the plant is already
   **bagged** before recording begins — deleted the "plant wilting in your other hand" clause;
   the recording is a separate step after collection.
4. **The integrator was you.** Each instrument gives one number in its own units; the collector
   has to read, note, and later stitch them into one record. The kit doesn't add up on its own.
   Kim: this line "places the responsibility exactly where it should land." It's also the plant
   for Ch. 3, where the phone becomes the integrator instead.
5. **The reframe — the constructive question.** Don't improve the tools (files down one friction,
   leaves the rest). Ask **what the record actually needs to hold.** That sorts the record into
   two kinds: **fixed by where you stand** (derivable, recoverable later) and **judged from what
   you see** (observed at the plant). Kim: likes stating this **"fixed by" / "judged from"**
   division here; it doesn't spoil Ch. 6, it's the right hinge.
6. **Alternatives fix a point, not the span.** Dictation (spares the hands, still transcribe
   audio, nothing sortable); an app with **built-in categories** (a real step, but head-down in
   a screen, nothing for the derivable context). "A point fixed is not a system built."
   NOTE (Kim, tentative): use **"built-in categories"** rather than "clean categories" for the
   app, to preview our controlled-vocabulary decks.
7. **Closing turn.** The book's real question: not which tool to carry, but what the record has
   to hold, and how little we can ask of the collector to secure it. "Everything that follows is
   worked out from there."
