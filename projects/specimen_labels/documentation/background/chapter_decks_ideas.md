## Threads
1. **What a deck is (concrete).** A small stack of cards for one kind of observation (phenology: flowering /
   fruiting / bud). "A deck is a question, and its cards are the answers you're allowed to give."
2. **The filter (the path-setting teaching).** A field earns a deck only if it is (observed, not fixed by the
   landscape) AND (categorical, not a measurement or sentence) AND (decided in the field, not at the desk).
   Walk the examples: date/coords fail (from the photo), elevation/slope fail (derived), family/determination
   fail (desk), habitat prose fails (the comment card). "The filter is the design; the decks are what survive
   it." (Teaching the method to decide, not handing a list = path not destination.)
3. **The three families.** Identity (number, target species), observation (the categorical judgments), the
   comment card (the writable escape hatch; the honest admission no deck anticipates everything).
4. **A deck = facet + value-set.** The **facet** (the question) travels; the **value-set** (the printed
   answers) is local to a flora/region (Hawaii lava/ash/cinder vs Georgia clay/alluvium/roadside). "You
   inherit the questions. You write the answers for where you work." (The creative-heart act = crafting the
   value-set.)
5. **Physical handling (form matches data).** Observation decks = screw-post stacks, color-coded covers,
   rotate ONE card into view (affordance enforces mutual exclusivity — flowering or not, never both). Identity
   + comment = business-card holders (detach / write). Fits a vest pocket. "The shape of the tool says how the
   tool is used."
6. **Why a deck beats memory.** (a) The checklist (surgery/aviation): fatigue drops the obvious; a list forces
   the essential few. (b) Recognition over recall: think-it-up fixates; choose-from-a-list just picks. "A deck
   guards what you would forget, and it spares you what you would rather not have to recall."
7. **★ Path, not destination (the close).** "I haven't given you the decks... on purpose." No one right set
   (systematist vs ecologist; rainforest vs roadside; now vs fifty years ago). The path — a way to find your
   own decks — is worth more, and it "reads its answer out of the accumulated record of your field" (→ Ch. 7).
   Hands to Ch. 6: "That half we don't card. We derive it."
