## Threads
1. **The return state (Kim's note).** You come home with two matched collections: **collected specimens, each
   tagged with a unique number**, and **photographs, one per tag**. The number is the join. "Every specimen
   has its picture, and every picture knows its specimen." (ACCURACY, Kim: at return it's a **collected**
   specimen, NOT yet "pressed" — pressing/mounting comes later and needs no describing. Corrected here and in
   Ch. 2, which also said "pressed plant.")
2. **Defer the reading to good conditions.** Nothing more happens in the field; the reading waits for the
   desk, "dry, warm, and unhurried... because the photograph kept everything." (Attention-friction payoff.)
3. **One photo → one record.** Decode every card, read EXIF (coordinate + time), derive the fixed variables
   from the coordinate → "one record: everything known about one specimen, gathered in a single structured
   place." (Kept at the architectural level; decode/derive mechanics are Ch. 3 / Ch. 6.)
4. **The record is the single source of truth.** "Not a label, and not a log, but the record they both come
   from." The pivot of the chapter.
5. **View one — the label** (per specimen). "One record, one specimen, one label."
6. **View two — the log row** (per trip). Gather the rows → the log, "the artifact that used to go missing."
7. **★ The honesty payoff (the chapter's real work; Kim liked it).** Label and log are two views of one
   record, not copies of each other, so they "can never disagree." The old world made each separately (copy
   errors, misremembered dates → silent divergence). "When both are just projections of a single capture,
   that whole family of error has nowhere to live. Two views, one truth."
8. **Name it + forward.** "capture once, project two views." → the next chapters fill the record (decks;
   derive). "The record is built. Now we furnish it."
