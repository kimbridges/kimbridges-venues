## Threads
1. **The reframe (the deliverable is the method).** The decks a field needs are "already written in that
   field's own record"; we read them out, we don't prescribe them.
2. **The crowd-source insight.** Two centuries of sheets = millions of small expert choices about what to
   record; nobody wrote the pattern down, but the choices survived and are now digitized and public. "A record
   of expert judgment, and it can be read back."
3. **★ The stance — peering over the experts' shoulders (Kim's Andes story).** Don't prescribe; watch what
   good collectors actually did, at scale. First-person anchor: lunch high in the Andes with the world's
   **best-known** ornithologists, condors turning; wanting to look through each expert's binoculars to see
   which glass they trusted; bought his own on their advice. "Discovering decks is that, done with data." (Kim
   owns this memory — expand/trim as he likes; he approved it "just right" + added "best-known".)
4. **The procedure (plain steps).** Pull records (taxon / region / recent decades) → consolidate to primary
   collector → per-collector field signature → cluster.
5. **What the groups mean.** Each cluster = a discipline exposed by its habits → its deck. A field almost
   nobody fills but everyone should = a shared gap (cross-cutting deck).
6. **Worked example — Metrosideros → four archetypes** (describers / place-recorders / terse / machine), none
   named in advance; **phenology the universal gap** = everyone's deck. **FIGURE:** the signature heatmap.
7. **Validation — kudzu answers differently** (describers vanish; mappers/monitors appear; phenology now
   recorded) → proof it's a method, not a one-off. "It hands back your community's answer."
8. **Path, not destination (payoff of Ch. 5).** Decks discovered fresh per flora and era; transmissible to a
   botanist working a flora Kim has never seen. "Deck design stops being a matter of opinion and becomes a
   matter of measurement."
9. **Close.** Both halves now held (observed = discoverable decks; fixed = derived from the coordinate pair);
   → Ch. 8, watch it run.
