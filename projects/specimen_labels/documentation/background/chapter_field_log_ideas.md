## Threads
1. **Label = security; field log = the new thing.** The label had to be right and now makes itself; the field
   log is "the genuinely new thing, the part that didn't exist before."
2. **The field log is CONTEXT, and context is visual.** Not a row; the context of the whole event, now
   holdable because a coordinate pair derives a landscape. "context no field botanist ever had... all of it,
   for nothing."
3. **The aggregate on the hillshade.** The collection as a picture on the real ground; "a single map shows at
   a glance." (FIGURE 1.)
4. **Scale.** Local slope → region → familiar features. "only a map at more than one scale can say that."
5. **★ Absence as data (Kim).** A map of where you collected is a map of where you didn't; answers "truly
   absent, or did nobody look?" "the whole difference between a real absence and an empty space on a map."
6. **The doors (suggestive, not exhaustive).** Canopy **physiognomy** (Kim's "blew me away"), **biome**
   (whittakerr), **seasonality + past-year weather** (seasonalityr), **specimen scan at pressing** (visual +
   dropped-tag backup). "Each is a door." (FIGURE 2 = the canopy physiognomy door.)
7. **The challenge.** "not to hand you a field log but to ask you for one"; the tools are new and will get
   better; some are "yours to invent."
8. **★ Circle closes (Linnaeus).** "I began in a small room in Sweden, asking how a person respects what was
   done there." Answer: carry the tradition forward with the best tools, hand it on richer. Echoes the Intro's
   last line: "The writing table and the cabinet have become a phone and an open map. The habit of mind is the
   same."
9. **Ends on the question** (voyage close): "What will you make of them?"

## ★ Illustration convention — "caption as door" (Kim, 2026-07-03)
Figures explained **only in their caption**, detailed enough to **state the result AND name how it was
obtained** (the method-pointer), so each illustration **motivates the reader to hunt down the methodology**
without a how-to in the body. Prose stays suggestive; captions carry the pointers. Fits path-not-destination +
"each is a door." **Applies across the book's figures** (hillshade, discover_decks heatmap, derived site,
canopy physiognomy). Canonical example — the **canopy physiognomy** figure: a canopy/satellite image; caption
gives the physiognomic reading (height/closure/structure) and notes it came **from an AI vision query** of the
image, the coordinate pair having come from one field photograph. (Also recorded in `design_notes.md`; consider
promoting to `style_multichapter_doc.md` §6 figures for future documents.)
