# Creamsicle, our 2016 Smart Car

# The Origin Story

We were getting increasingly worried. Nancy’s brother was having some health problems. It wasn’t a new problem. He’d been struggling for a while. His condition was getting worse. How bad? Very bad.

The treatment presents challenges for both the patient and caregivers. At the least, there needs to be two full-time helpers for several months. Add the importance of creating a clean, safe environment and supplying meals governed by strict dietary guidelines.

We volunteered to be the support team, if it was needed. In August, 2021, there was no certainty that Nancy’s brother would get the treatment he needed. Lots of factors were in play. 

Nancy went to Madison to check on her brother in early September.

We officially committed to being helpers. We’d need a car if we had to stay on the mainland for an extended period. Even if we stayed at her brother’s house, we would require a car. The complicating factor is that this would be in Wisconsin and we’re in Hawai\`i.

We figured there are two solutions to the car “problem.” We could rent a car or we could buy a used car (that we would sell later). The option of shipping one of our Hawai\`i cars to the mainland was ruled out early as being impractical.

The car-purchase option seemed like the better choice. Could we find the right car? It had to be a Smart car, of course.

It took a few hours of searching on the Internet to find a Smart car that met our specifications and was available at an appropriate location. We didn’t want to buy a car from Florida, for example, because of the distance we’d have to drive to get it where it was needed. 

On September 15, 2021, we wired $11,999 to Modern Motorcars, a dealer in high-end autos located in Nixa, a small city near Springfield, Missouri. They had just what we wanted and the location, while far away, was within our range.

We knew that a good GPS-based navigation system would be very useful, if not essential. A Garmin Drivesmart 65, along with a Garmin Mini 2 dash cam, were purchased at the Honolulu Best Buy. These would go to the mainland as part of the car pickup.

Insurance was purchased on September 18\. That completed the paperwork that was required before picking up the car.

# Keeping the Car

From the earliest considerations of buying a car we faced the need to have a place to store the vehicle when we were not using it. Nancy’s cousin, with exceptional generosity, said we could keep the car in her garage in SoCal. We’d need to help her clear out some unneeded stuff to make room. No problem. 

This was another key thing put in place.

Having a home base for the car set a schedule in place. The car would be picked up and driven back to Southern California. There, it would be registered and given a thorough checkout. Then, when appropriate, it would be driven to Wisconsin.

# Picking Up the Car

I went from Hawai\`i to LAX on a Delta flight on September 21, then continued on to  Kansas City. It was after midnight by the time I got to my hotel near the airport. 

I checked with Modern Motorcars in the morning and was told there was a slight delay. They were waiting on a motor mount. Could I wait one more delay before picking up the car? There wasn’t any option. I hung around the hotel while I waited on the repair.

On September 23, I boarded a bus to Springfield. On my arrival, I was met by a Modern Motorcars representative and driven to the dealership. As promised, all I had to do was sign a few papers. The Smart car was now mine. 

At that point, Nancy and I became owners of a *fleet* of Smart cars (2 in Hawai\`i, 1 on the mainland).

There was a small concern. The motor mount hadn’t arrived. But the mechanic decided that it wasn’t critical. I thought it would be something Madness Autoworks could address in California.

The car met all my expectations. It was spotless and well serviced. The color combination, white and orange, was perfect, too.

This Smart model is a 453 ForTwo. It’s a special edition, called the “Edition \#1.”

The engine is 0.9L turbocharged three-cylinder with 89 hp. That’s quite different from TwoRed’s engine. The body is different, too. This model is also a bit larger. It has a 3.9-inch wider track and it is four inches longer. It’s taller, having grown a half inch to a height of 61.2 inches.

There are other improved characteristics, such as a tighter turning radius. This car has a functioning cruise control. Perhaps most important, it has a new six-speed transmission.

I noted, for reference, the gas tank capacity is 8.7 gallons (this includes a reserve). The predicted highway mileage is 39 miles/gallon.

# Trip 1: Naxa to Los Angeles

I drove the 209 miles back to my hotel in Kansas City, taking my time as I adjusted to the slightly different handling of this model. Once there, I stopped at Target for a few supplies for the upcoming drive. 

It was a good day. Everything worked as expected. We got a car.

My brother lives near Kansas City in the small town of Richmond, Missouri. I drove there in the morning and spent about a half day with him and his wife. Then it was on to Lincoln, Nebraska. The day’s drive was a total of 258 miles and I didn’t arrive at my hotel until about 6 PM.

The route back to California took me across the Nebraska plains and then, with an abrupt transition, through Colorado’s Rocky Mountains. This segment includes one of the most beautiful highways in the US. This is followed by a winding route through some beautiful Utah hills. The last segment crossed the desert in Nevada (where I bypassed Las Vegas) and went on to Southern California. 

The drive included five overnight stops. I averaged 355 miles a day (including the short first car-pickup day) with a trip total of 1,987 miles. There were eleven fuel stops. 

This was a solo drive through some beautiful lands. I often wondered, while driving this route, if people who live in “flatland” areas of the country are aware of the splendor of these western areas. The varied and colorful topography, in particular, makes for memorable driving.

# Prepping the Car

The first order of business, once in Los Angeles, was to get the car properly registered. Before that could be done, it needed a smog check. This is a California requirement. Nancy’s cousin knew just where to go. It took just a few hours to complete both the smog check and registration. This finished the paperwork aspects of owning the car. The cousin really helped by letting us use her address as the “home” for the car.

The next stop was getting the car serviced. Art Domingues at Madness Autowork (aka Smart Madness) has been our Smart service hero since we took TwoRed for outfitting in 2010\. He did his things and gave the car a clean bill of health. That was a relief and it’s always good to get an independent appraisal of the status of a used car.

We like to keep some basic tools in the car especially for situations that might arise on long trips. Home Depot, in Signal Hill, supplied what we wanted. We made a few purchases to supplement the car’s toolkit. Little things that might be handy.

At this point, the car is now properly registered, serviced and equipped. It also has a home in California for when that’s needed.

I returned to Hawai\`i on a Delta flight on October 5th. Now the wait began. Would Nancy’s brother need our help? If so, when?

# Trip 2: Los Angeles to Madison

I returned to Los Angeles on a Delta flight on October 14th. Nancy went to Wisconsin on the same day. The plan was for me to drive the car and meet Nancy in Madison.

The trip began early the next morning. The route took me across California, Arizona and New Mexico, passing south of the Rockies. The passing views change from desert to mountains. There are unexpectedly nice scenes of mountains, especially once you pass Albuquerque. Seeing this stretch again was a good reminder of a different kind of landscape beauty.

Then up through Colorado to Nebraska, across Iowa. Long straight roads and a need to strategize to get the most distance and still be on good highways. Finally the North-East leg to Madison. This part of the trip had a character of its own and the topography changed and the farmlands became more intimate.

It took six days with an average of 366 miles a day. This was another solo trip. There was a lot to see along the route.

To be honest, the trip pushed my daily driving limits. Each day covered at least 300 miles. The longest one was 464 miles on the drive between Kingman and Albuquerque.

The car performed well. The cruise control made the drive much easier.

Nancy was still in Madison. This was the first time she would see the car. Almost at first sight it got its name, Creamsicle. Now, everyone agrees that this is a perfect name for this white and orange colored car.

# Life with Creamsicle in Wisconsin

Nancy’s brother was needing more and more help. Nancy had to return to Hawai\`i as she still had a busy medical practice. I remained in Madison to help. Creamsicle showed its value as I had to take Nancy’s brother to appointments and run shopping errands. 

Nancy closed her practice and returned to Madison. We were both needed as caregivers at that point. For several months we used Creamsicle extensively as we organized the brother's house and did cross-town appointment runs. We couldn’t have done without Creamsicle.

There were some driving challenges as this was Winter. Some days had deep snow with ice patches here and there on the roads. In general, Creamsicle handled the Winter conditions quite well. It would have been nice to have had better grip with the tires, but that was minor compared to how well we were able to get around.

# Trip 3: The Drive Back West

On January 18, since Nancy’s brother was doing well, we were able to leave on a trip to the West Coast. This would be the first time for Nancy to do a long drive in Creamsicle and the first in a long time that we’d be doing a cross-country drive together. More important, all the future trips would be with the two of us going together. Kim mostly drives and Nancy is the expert navigator. 

Memorable stops included seeing Lincoln-related sights in Springfield and visiting Kim’s brother in Missouri. 

An unusual side trip was a drive through the Rockies on a less-well-traveled highway. That got us to Santa Fe, New Mexico. We spent some time visiting the Georgia O’Keeffe Museum. 

We pushed on to the Grand Canyon. We got to see the sights. Then we finished the trip with the drive to Torrance. That last increment, when you’re tired and going across the LA Basin in heavy freeway traffic, is a challenge. But we made it\!

It was a good drive and it was a nice end to a long stay on the mainland. We were glad to help out a family member who was in need of assistance.

The distance was 2,464 miles. 

We parked Creamsicle in the cousin’s garage and returned to Hawai'i.

# Trip 4: West Coast to Oregon

The next time we took Creamsicle out was for a trip to see friends in Eugene, Oregon. This was late May and into June in 2022\. We drove up through California, stopping at a few favorite locations. We’re particularly attracted to the Coastal Redwood forests. The Lady Bird Johnson grove has a nice loop trail that gives you plenty of excellent big-tree moments. The nearby Fern Canyon was an interesting coastal preserve. Well worth a visit.

Seeing our Eugene friends is always interesting. This visit didn’t disappoint.

On the way back, we hugged the coast as much as we could. Our goal was to stay as close to the coast as possible. That was challenging as many of the roads were narrow and windy. A day of driving, even though it wasn’t a particularly long distance, was very tiring. The views made it worthwhile. This is one of those bucket list drives. Stop frequently to suck in the rugged scenes of mountain slopes plunging into the wild ocean waves. Offshore rocks add interest.

This was a 1,943 mile trip.

# Trip 5: West Coast to Washington

In November, 2022, we decided to do another West Coast trip. This time our goal was to do eagle photography in Washington, specifically in the area around Mt. Vernon, north of Seattle. On the way, we’d do some additional coast driving and explorations of the Olympic Peninsula.

We asked our Eugene friends to join us in Port Angeles as we’d arranged an Airbnb house for a few days. They drove up separately as we took a slower coastal route through Oregon and Washington while they could do the more direct freeway drive. 

We had a good time together exploring the Coastal Rainforests in the area. We were able to search for wildlife (yes, some eagles) and combine this with great forest views. Our stay included a visit to the Bloedel Reserve, one of our favorite botanical gardens. At this point, our friends returned to Eugene.

We were on our own again. The next stop was Mt. Vernon. This involved taking Creamsicle on the ferry. After some scouting for birds in the Squamish area and the nearby mountains, we found that this wasn’t really a good time to see eagles. But we discovered, quite by accident, that Short-ear Owls had returned to a specific Mt. Vernon field. It turned out that these birds were great photographic subjects. They more than made up for the sparsity of eagles.

We headed back South with a stop in Eugene. There, we had a nice Thanksgiving feast with our friends. What followed was a matter of driving as efficiently as possible back to Torrance. We skirted San Francisco and went down through central California on Highway 101\. We saw lots of vineyards. 

The overall distance was 3,061 miles.

# Trip 6: The Utah Loop

In March, 2023, we drove a loop from Torrance up through Nevada and into Utah and back. 

We visited Zion National Park first. Our arrival was very early in the season so there weren’t yet the huge crowds that usually fill this park. Our timing was good as the Winter travel difficulties were just over. One of the things people do is to hike up the canyon along the trail, then continue by walking in the river. The water level was low enough for us to start the water portion but good sense soon told us to turn back. We’re glad we did. 

We moved on to Bryce Canyon NP. There we found that Winter was still around. Snow and particularly ice made this visit treacherous. It was easy to slip en route to a viewing site. Some curious and brave ravens kept us amused.

There are a number of interesting and less well known parks in this region. We routed ourselves through these as we could. Eventually we got to Moab, the gateway to Arches NP. This was our second visit to this park there so we were prepared for what to expect. Entering the park early allowed us to avoid the long wait experienced by many people. We did a few hikes, including a rather spooky one to Delicate Arch. 

Visits to all these parks satisfied a long-standing bucket list item.

The trip back to Torrance involved an unusual detour. We chose to drive across Death Valley and then to spend some time at the foot of the Sierras in Lone Pine. We were able to drive back into Death Valley from this anchor location. This venue also gave us a chance to visit Manzanar, an internment camp in World War II. 

This trip was 2,355 miles.

# Trip 7: Vancouver Island

We decided that we needed to take another trip with our Eugene friends. We departed Torrance on September 8, 2023 with Canada as our destination, specifically Tofino, on the West Coast of Vancouver Island. We drove up the West Coast and did our usual stops at some favorite Coastal Redwood locations. Then on to Eugene for a brief stop. Then we headed north and our friends followed soon after we left. We needed to do this trip in two vehicles as we’d be staying in Canada a bit longer than our friends.

We met up with our friends in Port Angeles where we took the ferry across to Victoria, BC. This was a particularly nice stop as we were able to visit the Bouchard Gardens. We have visited lots of botanical gardens. This one had been on our to-do list for a long time. It didn’t disappoint and it is large, interestingly planned and well kept. Then it was off to Tofino where we had a very nice Airbnb house.

During our Tofino stay we were able to do more forest explorations. One morning we engaged a bird expert who took us bird watching. He found some great places to do bird spotting, mostly on the beaches and nearby forest areas. 

Our friends had to return to work in Oregon but we had some time so we went for a stay at Campbell River. It was there that we took a boat tour run by First Nation people to see bears and cultural sights. We got what we went to see.

The drive back included a stop in Vancouver to visit a friend. We crossed the border after a long wait in line at the custom’s point. We were scolded by the attendant; he told us we should have taken the line that had no wait.

A quick stop in Eugene. There we discovered our friends had both picked up Covid-19 during the trip so their drive home was a huge challenge.

Then down the coast with a stop in Monterey to see the aquarium, the drive along Highway 1, and finally a stop at Morro Bay to see the pelicans.

The distance was 3,447 miles.

# Trip 8: The Y-Y Loop

In mid-June, 2024, we took off on a trip that would take us to two of America’s top National Parks: Yosemite and Yellowstone.

Our first destination was Morro Bay. This was another chance to do Pelican photography. Then we cut across California on small highways to Yosemite National Park. 

We had a really good time seeing the sights in Yosemite. We used the bus to get around after we parked Creamsicle at a convenient location when we were in the valley areas. This gave us easy access to the waterfalls and other iconic viewing points. We walked a lot. The road up the south side of the valley provided us with additional perspective as we looked down on the park’s features. Having both close and far views helps explain why this park is a favorite.

We headed out by crossing the Sierras. The road goes through the forest, by some high-elevation lakes, then descends to the desert floor at Mono Lake. We drove a short distance south to spend the night at Mammoth Lakes. This stop prepared us for the upcoming long run across Nevada and Idaho. 

Crossing the northern end of the Great Basin Desert in Nevada has its own appeal. The first part of the next leg was on rural roads. That was a bit of a short cut, but also a good way to see this unusual desert country.  We picked up the Interstate for a short drive the next day. This was followed by a long day, half on the Interstate and half on rural roads until we were in Idaho. 

We were then within a day’s drive of West Yellowstone. On the way, we visited Minidoka, another of the internment camps. The visitor center was closed but we got to walk around and see the few remnants of this horrible government action.

Then we were in West Yellowstone. We used this as a gateway to Yellowstone National Park. It was convenient to make daily trips into the park from there. 

We saw all the usual geologic sights. This was our second visit. This time it was particularly good as we weren’t rushed. We spent a lot of time doing the boardwalks to see the variety in geysers. This included a stop at Old Faithful. We saw the spouting, of course, but also the visit of several buffalo. This wasn’t our only buffalo encounter. We saw lots of them as we drove along the park roads. This visit fully saturated our sensory systems.

The trip back included a stop at Grand Teton NP and a short stay at Jackson, WY. We were able to bypass Salt Lake City by going east of the Uinta Mountains. We continued heading back with a brief stop in Las Vegas and then on to Torrance.

This was a 2,571 mile loop. It gave us a lot of great memories as well as checked off a lot of bucket list items.

# Trip 9: Delivery to Nancy’s Brother

By this time, we had taken most of the trip we wanted to do in the Western US. There was also a need to vacate the cousin’s garage as she was ready to do some remodeling and needed the space.

Nancy’s brother’s car in Wisconsin was near the end of its practical life, so we decided that he could (should?) buy Creamsicle for a bargain price. He agreed that Creamsicle would suit his needs. On our part, we decided that we’d drive Creamsicle back to Wisconsin to deliver it to him.

The trip began on October 7, 2024\. Our route took us south through Arizona. We made a stop in Tucson so we could visit the Desert Museum and the Saguaro National Park. Then we headed to New Mexico and made a stop at White Sands NP. 

While we were in the area, we visited the nearby Carlsbad Caverns National Park. The walk down into the caverns is an amazing experience. It is larger than most people imagine. Once down at the bottom, the exploring continues as you walk around so many different geological formations. It is hard to describe this other-worldly place. We were glad that we chose this extreme southern route to start this trip.

We drove up through New Mexico and then across Oklahoma and into Missouri. For this leg of the trip we were mostly interested in making good time. 

Once we were near Kansas City, we had another chance to visit Kim’s brother and his family in Missouri. Then we headed north, going through Iowa and into Wisconsin. 

We made a short stop at the Wisconsin Dells so we could visit the International Crane Research Center. We’ve visited the cranes before but this time we got to see their newly renovated facilities. This is a much-improved way for them to keep and display these magnificent birds. 

Then, all we had left was the short drive south to Madison.

We delivered Creamsicle to Nancy’s brother on October 20th. This last trip was 2,699 miles.

# Overall

We had Creamsicle for just over three years. In that time, we drove 28,697 miles, or 9,845 miles/year on the average.
