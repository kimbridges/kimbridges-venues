## Verified facts (with corrections)
- AQI (EPA 2018 TAD, RGB): Good 0,228,0; Moderate 255,255,0; USG 255,126,0; Unhealthy 255,0,0; Very Unhealthy 143,63,151; Hazardous 126,0,35.
  40 CFR 58 App. G: Hazardous = '301 and above'; 'use only' these names and colours. (airnow.gov TAD 2018; law.cornell.edu)
- WHO UV Index (2002): Low green PMS 375; Moderate yellow PMS 102; High orange PMS 151; Very High red PMS 032; Extreme purple PMS 265.
  WHO: the colours have NO SCIENTIFIC BASIS -- chosen to make the presentation appealing; Australia's BoM does not follow them.
- NWS heat index names Caution / Extreme Caution / Danger / Extreme Danger; THRESHOLDS DIFFER between versions (80-89/90-104/105-129/130+ vs
  80-90/90-105/105-130/130+ vs 80/90/103/125) -- note for ch07_fig2. NWS HeatRisk: green / yellow / orange / red / MAGENTA.
- Japan WBGT levels: BLUE almost safe <21; sky blue caution 21-25; yellow warning 25-28; orange severe warning 28-31; red danger >=31.
- Avalanche: Europe (EAWS) Low/Moderate/Considerable/High/Very high, green-yellow-orange-red-RED/BLACK CHECKERBOARD; North America 2010
  ... High/EXTREME, green-yellow-orange-red-BLACK. EAWS voted NOT to rename 'Very high' to 'Extreme'.
- US fire danger (Smokey sign): Low green, MODERATE BLUE, High yellow, Very High orange, Extreme red. Australia (from 1 Sep 2022): MODERATE
  GREEN, High yellow, Extreme orange, Catastrophic red, plus 'No rating'.
- US Homeland Security Advisory System 2002-2011: Low green, Guarded blue, Elevated yellow, High orange, Severe red. Launched at YELLOW
  (12 Mar 2002); never lower than Elevated (CRS). RED once, 10-13 Aug 2006, UK-to-US flights only. Retired: 'unclear', 'little practical
  information' (Napolitano); NTAS from 26 Apr 2011.
- ANSI Z535: DANGER white on red ('will'); WARNING black on orange ('may'); CAUTION black on yellow; NOTICE white on blue (not a hazard to
  people). ISO 3864 (RAL): red 3001 prohibition/fire; yellow 1003 warning; blue 5005 mandatory; green 6032 safe condition.
- START triage: green minor; yellow delayed; red immediate; black expectant -- urgency order (red, yellow, green) is not the colour sequence.
- Hong Kong signals 1, 3, 8 (NW/SW/NE/SE), 9, 10 -- history: 1917 signals 1-7; 1931 1-10 (5-8 = four quadrants); late 1930s 2-4 dropped;
  1956 No.3 reintroduced; 1973 signals 5-8 renamed 8NW/8SW/8NE/8SE 'to avoid misunderstanding'. Rainstorm signals: Amber / Red / Black.
- Railway signals: 1841 Birmingham -- WHITE = safety, red = danger, GREEN = CAUTION. White dropped (broken red lens shows white; stray white
  lights mistaken for 'clear'); green = clear in Britain c.1893 (GWR 1895); yellow caution GCR 1917; New Haven RR green/yellow/red 1899.
  First traffic light 9/10 Dec 1868, Westminster (J. P. Knight; semaphore + red/green gas lamps; exploded 2 Jan 1869). Amber added 1920
  (William Potts, Detroit).
- Japan ao: first signal 1930 (US import, green); public said AO; 1947 law adopted 'ao'; bluish shade from a NATIONAL POLICE AGENCY notice in
  the early 1970s (NOT a Cabinet Order) -- as blue as possible within the international green range.
- Stock colours: mainland China and Taiwan red = UP, green = down; Korea red up, BLUE down; Japan mixed; Hong Kong reportedly Western (VERIFY).
- Ski runs: North America green circle / blue square / black diamond (NSAA 1968, from Disney's Mineral King designs; double black 1980s);
  Europe blue / red / black (+ green in France, Spain, UK; orange in parts of Austria/Switzerland; DSV, DIN 32912); Japan green / red / black.
  BLUE = intermediate in North America but EASY in Europe; only North America adds SHAPES.
- Mourning: black in the West; WHITE in China, Hindu India, Buddhist Thailand; Japan white until early Meiji, then black under Western
  influence; medieval French queens 'deuil blanc'. (Links to Kim's grandmother's black.)
- Colour-vision deficiency: ~8% of men, ~0.4% of women of European descent; 4-6.5% of Chinese and Japanese men (Birch 2012). MUTCD: red on
  top / left so position carries the meaning; Quebec / PEI shaped lights (being phased out).
- Evidence: Chapanis 1994 -- hazard perceived white < yellow < orange < red; agreement only on DANGER and red; WARNING vs CAUTION barely
  distinguished. Braun & Silver 1995 (Ergonomics 38:2207) -- same word more dangerous in red; 'DEADLY' in green less so. Christ 1975 review --
  colour helps search sometimes, hurts sometimes. No strong evidence colour is read FASTER than words in warnings.
- More: Torino asteroid scale (1999; 11 numbers in 5 colours); USGS volcano alerts (word levels for the ground, colours for aviation -- can
  differ); JMA kikikuru (2022: black added for level 5, purples merged); football yellow/red cards (Ken Aston, inspired by a traffic light after
  language confusion at the 1966 World Cup; first used 1970).
