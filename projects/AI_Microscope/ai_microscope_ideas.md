# AI Microscope — Ideation / Guiding Thesis
_Project: AI_Microscope_
_Started: 2026-07-22_

## The core framing (Kim, 2026-07-22)

What we are doing is **not the creation of a product** (a microscope
simulator). It is an **exploration of the process** of going from an
older technology — one that was, in its day, leading-edge — to the
*same product* rebuilt with today's leading-edge technologies.

The **philosophy of the original is the key.** The product exists to
teach a difficult skill: reading leaf anatomy — one of the most
boring lab assignments — in a way that makes students care about
*why* the structure matters, not learn it for its own sake. It is a
**supportive tool, not a test.**

It is easy to get **sidelined by current trends** — turning it into a
game, or automating teaching tasks like grading. Both were raised in
Will McClatchey's review, and both are rejected: they change what the
thing *is*. A game gets judged as a game; grading turns a support
into surveillance. Neither serves the original goal.

The 1989 goal was **strong but barely touched by the technology of
its time** — storage limits, the C/Prolog hardware split, canned
text, a serial cable between machines. The team knew what they
wanted; the machines could not yet deliver it.

The **purpose here** is to see whether we can release those
technology constraints and actually *feel* what it is like to pursue
the same strong idea inside a far richer technological environment.
The measure of success is that feeling — the experience of the
constraints lifting — more than a shipped, polished product.

## Implications we're already living

- The **exploration is the deliverable**; the eventual document is the
  account of it, not a manual for an app.
- "Richer environment" is not decoration. This session is itself an
  instance of the thesis: we could *measure* the color-map's flaws
  (359,791 colors, five phantom categories, 39% of pixels
  force-assigned) and redesign the identification substrate in an
  afternoon — the kind of diagnosis and iteration the 1989
  environment simply could not support.
- **Guardrails from the philosophy:** no game, no grading. Every
  feature is judged by whether it makes a hard skill more approachable
  and respects the student's time — e.g., abundance % as a
  findability *hint*, never a score.
