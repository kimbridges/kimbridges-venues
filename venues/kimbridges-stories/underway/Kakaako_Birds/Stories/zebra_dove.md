# Zebra Dove

*Geopelia striata* · introduced to O‘ahu, 1922

The small barred dove at your feet is the most easily overlooked bird in
Honolulu, for the simple reason that it is everywhere. It has become part of
the pavement. And, like the pavement, it goes unseen.

## Underfoot everywhere

The zebra dove is the most widespread dove on the islands, and it earned that
rank quickly. Introduced in 1922 — released on O‘ahu, Moloka‘i, and Maui,
reportedly through a Singapore bird dealer — it had spread to nearly all the
main islands within about fifteen years. Today, it occupies every
developed lowland there is. [V: lit] You see it on lawns, but just as readily
on sidewalks, on roadsides, and out in the open of a parking lot. Wherever
there is bare ground and a fallen seed. [V: Kim] Its very abundance is what
makes it invisible: a bird this common stops registering as a bird at all,
which is precisely the habit this book is trying to break.

## The brave little forager

Watch one and it earns back the attention. The zebra dove is a ground bird,
moving with a quick, deliberate, almost rodent-like scurry, head down, wholly
intent on finding the next seed or small insect. [V: Kim] [V: lit] It is brave
out of all proportion to its size. It will work a parking lot among shoes and
tires, letting people pass close without flushing. [V: Kim] At a good feeding
patch, the birds gather in numbers, more sociably than the larger spotted dove
of the next chapter. [V: Kim] And for all the bird's general mildness, the
males have another side: the cooing, bowing male is the territorial one, and
he will turn aggressive in a dispute over ground or a mate. [V: Kim]

## A singer in disguise

Here is the thing the background bird never tells you. In its Southeast Asian
homeland the zebra dove is not overlooked at all. It's a celebrated
songster, the *merbuk*, kept as a prized cage bird and entered in cooing
competitions judged on the beauty of its voice. [V: lit] In Honolulu it asks
for no notice whatsoever, but listen for it: that soft, rhythmic coo running
quietly under the day is this very bird. It does not call attention to itself;
it simply calls, low and steady. And an entire tradition, an ocean away, is
built on that sound. The most unremarkable bird in the park is a famous singer
who has chosen to keep quiet about it.

## Telling the two doves apart

This is the smaller of Honolulu's two everyday doves, and learning to name it
is half of an easy pair. The zebra dove is slim and long-tailed, finely barred
in black across the neck and the sides of the breast — the "zebra" that names
it — with a soft bluish-gray face. [V: lit] The bird in the next chapter, the
spotted dove, is distinctly bigger and wears a spotted collar instead of bars.
Bars or spots, small or large: split those two the way you learned to split
the finch from the sparrow, and one more corner of the lawn resolves back into
species.
