# White-rumped Shama

*Copsychus malabaricus* · introduced to O‘ahu, 1940

This is the hardest bird in the book to lay eyes on, and quite possibly the
finest voice in it. You will almost certainly hear it long before you see it —
if you see it at all.

## Heard before seen

The shama is uncommon in our stretch of town, and for a specific reason: it
wants dense shade and thick understory, the deep green tangle of a forest
floor. The open coastal corridor of lawns and canals offers very little of
that. [V: lit] You find it only where a real pocket of shade has been allowed
to survive. Mine is a huge banyan not far from the King Kamehameha statue,
where I have come upon one on several separate visits. [V: Kim] But do not be
surprised to find it absent when you go looking. It's not a common bird here,
and it keeps its own counsel. [V: Kim] The likeliest first sign of it is not a
shape at all but a sound: out of the shade, unexpectedly, a voice.

## The voice

And what a voice. The white-rumped shama is famous across Asia as one of the
great singers of the bird world, and a moment's listening tells you why. [V: lit]
The song is a varied run of loud, clear, flutelike whistles. Never a simple
phrase merely repeated, but a shifting, inventive line that keeps moving, often
stitched through with flawless mimicry of other birds. Its a song that surprises you
even as it delights you. [V: Kim] [V: lit] Loud, clear, unexpected. It carries
for hundreds of meters, and in the hush of a downtown courtyard, under a great
tree, it stops you where you stand. [V: lit]

## Brought for that song

The shama did not wander here. It was brought to O‘ahu in 1940 by the Hui Manu
Society, the very same group that gave Honolulu the red-crested cardinal a
decade or so before. [V: lit] But the two birds answer two different halves of
that society's purpose. The Hui Manu set out to import *colorful and melodious*
songbirds, and where the cardinal was chosen for its color, the shama was
chosen for its voice. [V: lit] Hold the two together and you can read the
society's whole intention in a pair of living birds: one for the eye, one for
the ear. Of all the birds in these pages, the shama is the one that was carried across
an ocean for its voice alone.

## The curious one

There is one more thing I carried away from those meetings under the banyan. The
shama struck me as a genuinely curious bird: when I finally found it and stood
still to watch, it seemed every bit as interested in looking at me as I was in
looking at it. [V: Kim] Rare, half-hidden, kept
to the few corners where the city still holds a little deep shade, it announces
itself by the very voice it was carried here to provide. And then, when at last
you track that voice into the green and find its singer, it turns and meets your
eye with what looks, unmistakably, like your own curiosity reflected back. Of
all the birds in this book, the shama may be the one that most rewards the thing
the whole book has been asking of you: learn to listen, follow the sound into
the shade, and a rare and beautiful stranger will look back at you. [V: Kim]
