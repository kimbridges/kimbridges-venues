## Sources (literature-confirmed claims)
- **Native; the lone native in the book.** Manu-o-Kū (*Gygis alba*), a native
  seabird of the tropical Pacific/Atlantic/Indian oceans. In the MAIN Hawaiian
  Islands it nests only on O‘ahu, only in Honolulu (common in the NW Hawaiian
  Islands). O‘ahu population State-listed as **threatened**. (DLNR; Hui
  Manu-o-Kū; Hawai‘i Birding Trails; birdsofhawaii.info.)
- **Honolulu's official bird (2 Apr 2007).** (DLNR; whiteterns.org.)
- **Urban nesting history.** First documented Honolulu breeding 1961 — a pair in
  a kiawe tree near Hanauma Bay; population has grown steadily since. Recent
  Hui Manu-o-Kū survey: breeding adults ~3,600 (≈1.5× the 2016 count; >50% rise
  in a decade). Citizen active-nest map. (DLNR; Hawai‘i Public Radio 2026-04-02;
  whiteterns.org.)
- **No nest.** Lays a single egg on a bare branch — a fork or the cup left by a
  trimmed-limb scar — or on ledges/structures; chick hatches ~1 month. Well-
  pruned street trees create ideal cups. (Hawai‘i Public Radio; DLNR; Wikipedia.)
- **Feeding/flight.** Ranges up to ~120 mi offshore for small fish and squid
  driven up by larger predators (tuna); carries multiple small fish crosswise
  in the bill. Graceful, acrobatic flight; striking black bill, all-white
  plumage, dark eye-ring. (DLNR; papahanaumokuakea; Wikipedia.)
- **Protection.** Migratory Bird Treaty Act (since 1918); State-threatened;
  Hui Manu-o-Kū volunteers tie ribbons on nest trees to warn trimmers and to
  track birds. (Hui Manu-o-Kū; Hawai‘i Public Radio.)

## MAJOR framing note (flag to Kim)
This bird **reframes the whole book.** The other 11 species are all introduced
(six arrival vectors); the white tern is **native** — it needed no vector. So
the collection is best described not as "introduced birds" but as **urban birds
of Honolulu/Kaka‘ako — eleven introduced, one native** — with the native as the
deliberate capstone. Implications:
- The **preface / vector thread** should acknowledge the exception: every bird
  was brought here *except this one*. The white tern is the answer to the
  "how did it get here?" question being "it didn't — it's from here."
- The **two-Hui-Manu convergence** resolves here: the historical Hui Manu
  Society (introduced birds for beauty) vs the modern Hui Manu-o-Kū (protects
  this native). Earlier flagged as a "don't confuse" note in
  front_matter_notes.md; now it pays off in the finale.
- The **watched-invasion thread inverts:** the white tern is also a recent urban
  colonizer (since 1961, growing) — but a NATIVE moving into the city on its own,
  a homecoming rather than an invasion, welcomed rather than tolerated.
- **Order = 12, the closer.** Spotted dove (11) becomes the penultimate; its
  "always more to see" coda still works leading into this finale.
