## Sources (literature-confirmed claims)
- **Endemic + endangered.** *Anas wyvilliana*, koloa / koloa maoli ("true/native
  duck"). ENDEMIC to Hawai‘i (found nowhere else); FEDERALLY ENDANGERED. (DLNR
  Koloa Maoli; USGS koloa factsheet; Pacific Rim Conservation; Wikipedia.)
- **Looks like a female mallard.** Both sexes mottled brown, blue speculum
  edged white; pure koloa darker and **20–30% smaller** than mallards (males
  ~20", females ~16–17"). (DLNR; Wikipedia; mybirdbuddy.)
- **Hybridization = the gravest threat.** Interbreeds with introduced feral
  mallards → "genetic extinction." On O‘ahu MOST koloa-looking ducks are now
  koloa–mallard hybrids; pure (non-admixed) koloa survive mainly on **Kaua‘i**
  (largest pure population). Maui & others largely hybrid too. Management irony:
  mallards were NOT removed before O‘ahu reintroductions → drove the
  hybridization. (USGS factsheet; Lavretsky et al. / PubMed 31736171; The
  Wildlife Society; Conservation Genetics; DLNR.)
- **Other threats.** Habitat loss (~31% of coastal-plain wetlands lost in ~110
  yrs); predation by feral cats, rats, mongooses (eggs/young); historic
  hunting. (DLNR; Wikipedia.)
- **Habitat.** Wide range of natural + artificial freshwater wetlands (marshes,
  flooded grasslands, coastal ponds, streams, taro/lotus/shrimp/fish ponds);
  depends on FRESH water; **rarely in brackish/saline** areas. (DLNR; Pacific
  Rim Conservation.) → note vs Kim's brackish tidal canal: the canal is
  marginal/only-sometimes-fresh habitat; handled honestly in the draft.

## Kim-observed anchors / frame [V: Kim]
- A duck or two paddles by at the Ala Moana canal; looks mallard-like to anyone
  used to mainland birds, but it's different.
- Little duck habitat in Kaka‘ako; not ocean birds (not in the surf); a few
  mostly man-made spots — the State Capitol pond, areas by the Zoo, and the
  canal (the magnet). Small area, home to a few birds.
- Threats: habitat limits; food/hunting (most cuisines have a place for duck);
  and — the subtle, less-suspected one — genetics: interbreeding with introduced
  mallards; genetic dilution; a conservation-management concern. (The very
  ducks a visitor mistakes for mallards.)

## Thematic notes
- **The ENDEMIC — the deepest tie to the place, and the most threatened.**
  Unlike the indigenous natives (white tern, ‘auku‘u, kōlea, ‘akekeke, all found
  elsewhere too), the koloa is found ONLY in Hawai‘i. Strongest "found nowhere
  else" bird → a powerful NEAR-FINALE for the native section (decide order in
  the reorder; white tern still the official-bird finale, but the koloa may be
  the emotional climax just before it).
- **The book's clearest "fracture" (Kim's preface theme):** an outcome with no
  villain, harming the bird and implicating everyone — genetic erasure by a
  well-meant/careless release. The even-eye ethic at its most demanding.
- **Cruel-resemblance irony ties to the ID-skill thesis** (the house-finch
  chapter): here the look-alike problem is not a birding puzzle but a matter of
  survival — the resemblance that fools the eye is the resemblance that erases
  the species.
- **Ties to the night heron** (same Ala Moana canal; both native; both on the
  urban water) — keep nearby in the native section.
