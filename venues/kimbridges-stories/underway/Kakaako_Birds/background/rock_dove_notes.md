## Sources (literature-confirmed claims)
- **Introduction ~1796.** *Columba livia* introduced to Hawai‘i around 1796;
  feral populations derived from domesticated stock; common on all main
  islands; the familiar large-flock bird of downtown Honolulu. Native to
  Europe, Africa, Asia — a cliff/escarpment bird of rocky seacoasts and desert
  oases. Earliest of this book's four birds (vs mynah 1865, egret 1959, saffron
  finch ~1965). (Bishop Museum RLP monograph ROPI.pdf; to-hawaii.com; Birds of
  the World *Rock Pigeon*.)
- **City-as-cliff adaptation.** Building ledges, plazas, and bridges mimic the
  ancestral sea-cliff habitat — the urban niche is a match, not a degradation.
  (Birds of the World; general ornithology.)
- **Feeding law.** Hawai‘i state law (signed by Gov. Abercrombie, ~2013) makes
  *excessive* feeding of feral birds a public-health nuisance: feeding in a
  manner a reasonable person would expect to cause 25+ feral birds to linger/
  roost/congregate within a 20-ft radius. First offense fine; repeat within 5
  years a petty misdemeanor. Exempts permitted carrier/racing/show pigeons.
  Enforcement limited — reportedly no citations issued. (Hawaii News Now; Civil
  Beat; HB576 text.)
- **Plumage variation.** Feral pigeons descend from many domestic breeds, so a
  flock shows wide plumage variety (blue-bar, checker, rusty, pied, white) —
  unlike a uniform wild species. Iridescent neck feathers. (General; Birds of
  the World.)

## Two more Kim observations (added to draft 2026-06-04)
- **The white flock (1960s).** [V: Kim] Kim remembers Honolulu's pigeon flocks
  in the 1960s as *pure white* — every bird — and a white flock was "something
  to behold." Over the following years the ordinary colors entered and the
  flock became today's mottled mix. Kim was sorry to see it; the early mixtures
  were less attractive than the white, and the "motley" look likely added to
  the species' disfavor. Now interesting colors abound, some less pleasing.
  - **Verified mechanism context [V: lit].** Feral pigeons descend from many
    domestic breeds; a white founding stock would diversify as the gene pool
    mixed. Notably, the naive expectation — that feral flocks revert to the
    ancestral blue-bar over generations — does NOT hold: although blue-bar
    birds suffer less peregrine predation (real selection toward wild-type),
    city flocks stay strikingly varied and *why* is an open question
    (disassortative mating, introgression of melanistic/NDP alleles). So Kim's
    white→motley shift is a real, observed instance of feral-pigeon color
    dynamics that biologists still puzzle over. (Nature *Sci Reports* 2024;
    Feral Pigeon Project; PMC introgression studies; Wikipedia *Feral pigeon*.)
  - **Soft inference (lead):** the *specific* reason Honolulu's 1960s flock was
    white (a white founding/released stock) is reasonable but not documented
    for Honolulu — phrased cautiously in the draft. [V: lead]
- **Lawn dwellers vs. scavengers.** [V: Kim] Away from feeders, rock doves
  behave like any seed-eater — working a lawn for grass seed, not scavenging
  trash. Focus on the lawn birds and the disfavor lifts; the trash-pile bird
  and the lawn bird are the same species in two settings, and the feeders
  manufacture the first one (and the bad reputation). Woven into "A flock is a
  mirror."
