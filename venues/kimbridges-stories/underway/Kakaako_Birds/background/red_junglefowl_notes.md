## Sources (literature-confirmed claims)
- **Moa = Red Junglefowl (*Gallus gallus*)**, the wild Southeast Asian ancestor
  of every domestic chicken. Hawaiian name **moa**, still in use. (Bishop
  Museum RLP monograph REJU.pdf — "Other: Moa, Wild Chicken"; Hawai‘i Birding
  Trails; Images of Old Hawai‘i.)
- **Polynesian canoe animal.** Brought by Polynesian voyagers on the founding
  canoes with pigs, taro, and the rest of the "canoe plants and animals"; for
  food, eggs, feathers, and ceremony. Long before European contact. (Images of
  Old Hawai‘i; Hawai‘i Guide; National Geographic.)
- **Date.** Roughly a thousand years ago; sources range ~500–700 AD to
  ~1219–1266 AD ("over 800 years ago" / "at least 1,000 years"). Use a soft
  "roughly a thousand years ago / long before European contact." This makes moa
  the OLDEST introduced bird in the book by ~8 centuries (vs rock dove 1796).
- **Today's hybrid.** Feral chickens are a genetic blend: ancestral Polynesian
  moa + 20th-century domestic breeds that escaped/were released, notably during
  Hurricanes Iwa (1982) and Iniki (1992). 2022 genetic study (Heredity) dates
  the admixture ~1981 (1976–1995). Famous on Kaua‘i; common on O‘ahu too.
  (Heredity 2022 / PMC; National Geographic; Modern Farmer; Hawai‘i Guide.)

## Kim's frame (the chapter's spine) [V: Kim]
"We all call it a chicken, but that's misleading — it obscures the cultural
roots." The deepest cultural roots of any bird here. The voyaging setup: think
about the isolation; how would a voyager prepare for a trip into the unknown
ocean? Food for the trip, and founding stock for the generations to come if a
new homeland is found. The first discoverers did this. "They had a name for
this bird" → the reveal: **moa.**

## Thematic notes
- **Deepest layer of the immigration synthesis.** The moa is the FIRST
  immigrants' bird (Polynesian voyagers), a thousand years before the
  Western-era introductions. It pushes the book's "introduced birds mirror the
  people who came" frame back to the very first human arrival — and stands as
  the counterpart to the White Tern (the native/place): moa = the first people;
  white tern = the place that was already here. Update front_matter_notes vector

- **"Chicken" vs "moa"** is itself the book's method in miniature: renaming the
  familiar bird reveals the culture behind it ("learning about a bird is
  learning about yourself").
- **The hybrid = a "fracture/layers" story** (Kim's preface theme): the living
