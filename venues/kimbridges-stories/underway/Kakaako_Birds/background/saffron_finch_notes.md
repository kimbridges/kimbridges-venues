## Sources (literature-confirmed claims)
- **Introduction ~1965, from escaped/released cage birds.** Established from
  cage birds; finches released at Pu‘u Wa‘a Wa‘a Ranch (Big Island) in or
  before 1965, increasing by Dec 1967, "abundant" there 1969–71; first noted
  on O‘ahu 1965. Native to South America; a popular cage bird for color and
  song. (Bishop Museum RLP monograph SAFI; birdsofhawaii.info; Wikipedia
  *Saffron finch*.)
- **O‘ahu spread timeline.** Sporadic around Honolulu in the 1990s (Kapi‘olani
  Park, Salt Lake/Moanalua, Hawai‘i Kai); 2000s in Waipahu, Mililani, ‘Ewa
  Beach, Nānākuli; early 2010s in Nu‘uanu Valley and N shore toward Hale‘iwa.
  Now locally common on O‘ahu (e.g. Waikīkī, West Loch); abundant Big Island
  lowlands; scarce on Kaua‘i (mainly Waimea). (Hawai‘i Birding Trails;
  birdsofhawaii.info.)
- **Habitat.** Short grassy areas / open lawns along the coast; urban parks.
  Fits Kaka‘ako Waterfront Park lawns. (Hawai‘i Birding Trails; Wikipedia.)
- **Field marks.** Male bright yellow with distinct orange crown wash; female
  duller, more olive-brown; juveniles buff gray-brown with some yellow. (Old
  PDF stub + birdsofhawaii.info.)

## Cage-bird thread (Kim's curiosity, 2026-06-04)
Kim asked: why was a caged saffron finch on the Big Island, and can we buy
cage birds now? Worth developing as a thread.
- **Why it was there.** Pu‘u Wa‘a Wa‘a / Pu‘u Anahulu ranchland was "ground
  zero" for intentional exotic-bird release in the early–mid 1900s, especially
  finches, waxbills, and gamebirds — lavender waxbill, red-cheeked cordon-bleu,
  black-rumped waxbill, white-rumped seedeaters (1966), southern masked-weaver
  (1966), all logged as "released cage birds." Most failed to establish;
  saffron finch is a rare success. So the vector is **private aviculture**, a
  third route distinct from the mynah's and egret's government programs.
  (Bishop Museum RLP Non-Established Species List; Hawai‘i Birding Trails;
  birdfinding.info Pu‘u Anahulu.) [V: lit]
- **Can you buy cage birds now?** Yes — finches, canaries, parakeets, parrots,
  cockatiels are generally allowed as pets. BUT importing to Hawai‘i needs a
  Plant Quarantine Import Permit + a Poultry and Bird Permit, plus a 7-day
  (168 hr) pre-arrival isolation (West Nile precaution). Many species
  prohibited (lories, monk parakeets, toucans, bulbuls, etc.). The casual
  aviculture that seeded the saffron finch is now precisely what the state
  guards against — a clean irony. (HI Dept of Agriculture, Animal Industry /
  Plant Quarantine; hawaiilife.com pet-law summary.) [V: lit]
- **Scope question for Kim:** keep this as the compact paragraph now in the
  saffron finch chapter, OR grow it into a book-level "how they got here"
  thread paralleling the Hui Manu — two vectors, the aesthetic songbird
  society and the cage-bird trade. See front_matter_notes.md.

## Thematic note
Three arrival modes across the book's first three birds, a built-in thread:
mynah (1865, deliberate pest control) → egret (1959, deliberate bio-control)
→ saffron finch (~1965, accidental escape of a cage bird). The saffron finch
is also the only one whose O‘ahu range is still actively expanding — a
colonization observable in living memory. Good "domain layer" for the chapter.
