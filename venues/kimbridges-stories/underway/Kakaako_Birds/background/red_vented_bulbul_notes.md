# Red-vented Bulbul — working notes

Background and follow-up for `Stories/red_vented_bulbul.md`. Same rules:

## Sources (literature-confirmed claims)
- **Introduction mid-1950s, escaped/released cage birds, O‘ahu.** Native to
  southern Asia (Pakistan/India to China); popular cage bird; brought to O‘ahu
  and illegally released in the mid-1950s. DATE-GUARD: a ~1965 date that
  sometimes attaches to "bulbul" refers to a *different* species, NOT this one —
  keep the mid-1950s date. That other species is **excluded from this book**
  (Kim, 2026-06-05: not seen in the area, not its habitat); do not re-introduce
  it. (To-Hawaii.com; Hawai‘i Early Detection Network; HISC; Bishop Museum
  monographs; Wikipedia.)
- **Pest status.** Eats commercial fruit (papaya, mango, lychee, banana),
  vegetables, and flower/orchid buds, plus insects; O‘ahu orchid industry
  reported ~$300,000 in losses one year; disperses invasive-plant seeds
  (miconia, ivy gourd). On the **IUCN 100 worst invasive species** list and the
  **State of Hawai‘i Injurious Species list** — illegal to release, transport
  interisland, or export without a permit. Established O‘ahu; not established on
  other islands, but can nest in shipping containers and fly channels (MISC /
  invasive-species committees on alert; Maui sightings 2021, 2025). (HISC; MISC;
  Maui Now; Wikipedia.)
- **Field marks.** Dark, slightly spiky-crested head; scaled brown body; white
  rump; red patch under the tail (the "vent"). Active, conspicuous, vocal.

## Kim-observed anchors [V: Kim]
- **Never still.** An intensely active bird; never seems to perch quietly.
- **Friend/foe duality.** Eats insects (beneficial — "your friend") AND fruit
  (one bite ruins a papaya — "not so much"); disliked by backyard fruit growers
  and plantation owners alike. This duality is the chapter's spine.
