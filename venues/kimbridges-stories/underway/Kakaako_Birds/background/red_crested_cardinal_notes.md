# Red-crested Cardinal — working notes

## Sources (literature-confirmed claims)
- **Introduction ~1928–1931, primarily by the Hui Manu Society.** Introduced to
  O‘ahu and Kaua‘i 1928–31 (O‘ahu ~1928), then spread slowly through both and
  naturally to other Southeastern Hawaiian islands; now common on O‘ahu and
  Maui. (Bishop Museum RLP monograph RCCA.pdf; Hawai‘i Birding Trails;
  birdsofhawaii.info.) **This is the Hui Manu thread's first species payoff.**
- **Native range.** Northern Argentina, Bolivia, southern Brazil (Rio Grande
  do Sul), Paraguay, Uruguay. Also called Brazilian Cardinal. (Wikipedia;
  To-Hawaii.com.)
- **Not a true cardinal.** Paroaria coronata is a **tanager** (Thraupidae), not
  a Cardinalidae cardinal — the name is by resemblance. (Wikipedia; BotW.)
- **Behavior.** Usually in pairs, on lawns and in trees; adapts well to human
  disturbance; described as a fairly **aggressive / bold** species. (Hawai‘i
  Birding Trails; hawaiiislandkauai.com.) NOTE: the literature frames it as
  bold/human-tolerant, NOT "tame" or "curious" — see verification nuance below.

## Kim-observed anchors [V: Kim]
- **The pair that approaches.** In Kaka‘ako Waterfront Park, a pair will often
  approach a person who stands still, come quite close, and pause to look — as
  if curious. Almost always a pair.
- **"Missing color."** The bright red crown + pure white breast brought a color
  the local seed-eater flock had lacked (browns/yellows/grays). Converges
  beautifully with the Hui Manu's color-introduction purpose — they brought it
  for the red; Kim later noticed that red filling the gap.
- **First-sighting / "watched it invade."** Kim's first RCC was beside the
  **Waikiki Aquarium**; it startled him (he knew the urban birds; this one
  didn't fit). Second species he has watched move into Honolulu (saffron finch
  is the first) — directly demonstrates the book's search-image thesis (the new
  bird that breaks the learned pattern).
- **Loose pair, occasional mixing.** Most often a pair moving loosely together;
  sometimes mixed with other species.

## Verification nuance (handle in prose)
Kim experiences the approach as curiosity; the literature calls the species
bold, human-tolerant, even aggressive. These are not contradictory (a bold
bird approaches), but the DRAFT must not assert "curiosity/tameness" as a
documented trait. Resolution used: Kim's approach experience tagged [V: Kim];
the bold/confident framing tagged [V: lit]; the word "curiosity" attributed to
the watcher's-end impression, not to the bird's documented temperament.
