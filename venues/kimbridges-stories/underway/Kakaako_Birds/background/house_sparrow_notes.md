## Sources (literature-confirmed claims)
- **Introduction 1871.** Nine House Sparrows reached O‘ahu from New Zealand in
  1871; by 1879 had "the chief place" in Honolulu; by 1888 a "firm footing" on
  O‘ahu. Native to Eurasia / North Africa. Also called English Sparrow.
  (Bishop Museum RLP monograph HOSP.pdf; birdsofhawaii.info; explorebiodiversity.)
- **Deep human-commensalism.** The house-sparrow commensal lineage split ~11
  Kya and expanded ~6 Kya, tracking the Neolithic spread of agriculture;
  now virtually worldwide via that human association. Genome carries
  signatures of human-commensalism; commensal sparrows differ in beak/skull
  shape, adapted to human grain. (Royal Society Proc B 2018 "Signatures of
  human-commensalism…"; PMC beak/skull-shape study; Audubon; Wikipedia
  *House sparrow*.)
- **Bold scavenging / following people.** Exploits virtually any human food —
  café crumbs, insects off car grilles, fast-food dumpsters; the common thread
  is human settlement. (Wikipedia; eLife model-organism review.)
- **Male plumage.** Gray crown, black bib/throat, chestnut nape behind the eye;
  females/juveniles plain buff-brown. (General field guides.)

## Kim-observed anchor [V: Kim]
- **The picnic thief.** Leave a plate unattended and bold individuals dart from
  nearby perches to carry off scraps seemingly too big for a small bird. The
  key contrast with the pigeon: NO ONE is feeding the sparrows — they are "tiny
  thieves," taking uninvited, whereas the pigeon flock is fed into being. This
  contrast is the chapter's spine.
- **Park lawns.** Found in lawns in small flocks with other seed-eaters. Ties
  to the basic-set / learn-to-see thesis.

## Thematic notes
- **Vector: deliberate 19th-c. introduction** (the 1871 shipment), like the
  mynah — an intentional acclimatization-era introduction. Predates the Hui
  Manu (1930); **Hui Manu silent.** Could note alongside the mynah as the
  "deliberate Victorian introduction" pair if the front matter wants it.
- **Pigeon ↔ sparrow pairing.** Two intimate urban commensals, opposite
  relationships to us: the pigeon we feed (we make the flock), the sparrow
  steals (it helps itself). Worth keeping the two chapters near each other and
  letting the sparrow chapter call back to the pigeon (it already does).
- **Thesis fit.** Another "so common it's invisible" bird — the learn-to-see
  argument again; the close turns on noticing the overlooked male's markings.
