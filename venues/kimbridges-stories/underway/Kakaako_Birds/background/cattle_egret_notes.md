## Sources (literature-confirmed claims)
- **1959 introduction.** 105 cattle egrets imported from southern Florida,
  Jul–Aug 1959, by the Territory Board of Agriculture and Forestry with the
  Hui Manu Society, to control flies/ticks on cattle. Released in small groups
  on five islands; 16 held at Honolulu Zoo, released 1961. Population ~30,000
  statewide by the early 1980s. (Bishop Museum RLP monograph; HI Audubon
  *‘Elepaio*; USDA PSW / Paton; tsusinvasives.org.)
- **Commensal foraging / machinery.** Evolved following African megafauna;
  flushes-and-snatches; ~3.6× higher capture success near a large mover than
  alone; readily substitutes tractors, mowers, even aircraft for animals
  ("leap-frog feeding"). (Cornell Lab All About Birds; HeronConservation;
  Wikipedia *Cattle egret*.)
- **Kahuku prawn ponds.** Aquaculture ponds at Kahuku, O‘ahu are a favored
  feeding site; egrets prey on prawns at significant monetary loss to farmers.
  (Hawai‘i Forest & Trail; HI Audubon *‘Elepaio*.)
- **Native-waterbird impact / invasive status.** Nest predator on koloa, ae‘o
  (Hawaiian stilt), ‘alae ‘ula (Hawaiian moorhen), ‘alae ke‘oke‘o (Hawaiian
  coot); 2017 migratory-bird control order named the cattle egret. (tsusinvasives;
  Hawai‘i Public Radio Manu Minute; UH Hilo *Kekalahea*.)
